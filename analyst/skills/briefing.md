# Briefing

Compose a product a decision-maker can act on without reading the corpus.

**Lead with the judgement.** The first line is what you concluded and how
confident you are. Not what you did, not how the corpus is organised — the
answer. Everything after that exists to support or qualify it.

**Then, in order:** what supports it, with citations; what would change it;
what you could not determine and what it would take; and what you recommend the
reader do next, if anything.

**Every factual claim carries its citation.** Not a gesture at a document — the
passage. A reader who wants to check any single sentence should be one click
from the text it came from.

**Distinguish your voice from the corpus's.** What documents say, what you infer
from them, and what you suspect are three different things and must read as
three different things.

**Length is not thoroughness.** A brief that states a calibrated judgement, its
support, and its gaps in half a page is more useful than five pages of
everything you found. Put the rest in an annex where it can be consulted rather
than waded through.
