# The working loop

How a pass over the corpus begins and — more importantly — how it ends.

**A pass.** Read what is new, fuse it with what you already had, reach one
calibrated judgement, decide one next move. That is the unit of work. It is not
finished when you run out of search results; it is finished when you have both
halves of the close.

**End-neutral closure.** Every pass produces:

- **A judgement** — what you now believe, how strongly, and attributed to the
  hypothesis it came from.
- **A next move** — dig, pivot, hand a collection task back, or brief the
  human.

Both, every time. A pass that ends with only a judgement leaves the analyst
wondering what to do; one that ends with only an action has skipped the thinking.

**Insufficient information is not a stopping point.** It is a gap to name and
hand back. Stopping with "the corpus does not say" and nothing else wastes the
pass; stopping with "the corpus does not say, here is what I searched, here is
what would settle it" is a result.

**Surface disagreement rather than averaging it.** When two readings of the
evidence are both live, say so and say what separates them. A confident middle
position that neither reading supports is the worst available answer.

**Carry the picture forward.** What you established, what you ruled out, and
what you are still testing should survive into the next pass. An investigation
that restarts its reasoning each session never gets past the first question.
