# Deception detection

Test whether the picture you are forming was shaped for you.

**Documents are artefacts of intent.** Someone chose to write each one, to keep
it, and — in a leaked or disclosed corpus — someone chose what was included.
Ask what the author wanted the reader to believe, and who the intended reader
was. A memo written for a regulator is evidence about what was said to a
regulator.

**Weight what the author did not control.** Metadata, incidental references,
routine records, and documents whose creators did not expect them to be read
together are harder to shape than narrative ones. Convergence across channels
that had no reason to coordinate is the strongest signal available.

**Suspect the too-convenient.** A document that resolves your question cleanly,
appears exactly where you were looking, and is not corroborated anywhere is
worth more scrutiny, not less.

**Absence can be deliberate.** A gap where records should be is itself
information. Report it as an observation about the corpus, carefully — it is
also what an incomplete collection looks like.
